Distortion fit#

How the distortion of ESIS-I is fit to the flight data, and how to reproduce the fit committed to the package. The numbers in the tables below are read from the committed tables when this page is built.

What the data determine#

The terms fit are chosen by what each does to the image on the sensor. In each channel the yaw and pitch of the grating translate the spectrum, and with it every line’s window; the ruling spacing sets the dispersion; the pitch and yaw of the pointing translate the Sun within the windows; the sensor’s distance along the beam sets the plate scale; and its three angles rotate the image and tilt it in the two keystone senses. The other placements repeat one of these motions or leave the image unchanged to first order, and are held at their as-built values. The roll of the field stop moves only the edges of the windows. The displacement of the primary trades against the focus-preserving distance of the sensor. The in-plane position of the sensor trades against the grating’s yaw and pitch, and both trade against the pointing: the mapping cannot tell a moved sky from a moved grating. A global search over a set that contains such a null direction lands somewhere along it at random, and two seeds disagree by tens of pixels; over the nine terms, two seeds agree to two hundredths in merit.

Two things break the degeneracies the frame leaves. The edges of the windows are the image of the field stop through the optics, and do not move when the pointing does, so they say where the grating and sensor put the image and leave the pointing to explain the rest. Measured in every frame of the flight, they move by up to a pixel and a half end to end while the pointing sweeps seven arcseconds, or nine pixels, and the motion of all four channels’ windows is smooth through the flight and carried per frame. And the channels compared with one another on the sky resolve a tenth of a pixel where the proxy scene resolves one.

One more motion is visible only in the channels against one another. Each channel views the solar image through its own sector of the primary, so if that image is not exactly at the field stop, each channel sees it shifted along its own dispersion by the sector’s offset times the defocus, while the stop’s edge, an aperture in the plane the grating images, stays put: 1.6 pixels per 0.1 mm for ESIS-I. A change of the focus through the flight therefore separates the channels’ skies without moving their windows, in a pattern no pointing or grating motion can make.

What the data do not determine is held rather than fit. The angle between the windows and the sky, which a roll of the instrument or of the field stop would set, changes the merit by less than three thousandths over four degrees and is held at the design. The instrument roll is held at zero, in the reference and in every frame: the merit cannot measure a roll to an arcminute. The displacement of the primary is held at nominal.

The merit#

The fit compares an image of the Sun seen by another instrument (the AIA proxy scene, esis.flights.f1.data.synth.scene_aia()) with a Level-1 frame, after imaging the scene through the model of one channel. The image is made by esis.optics.LinearMerit: the channel is linearized (optika.systems.SequentialSystem.linearize()) with its field of view carried as the polygon its stop rays trace, so that every scene cell outside the field stop is blocked before the lines are summed, and the scene is regridded conservatively onto the sensor. The merit is the Pearson correlation with the frame.

The edge pixels are 0.37 % of a window. Moving only the edges by a pixel changes the correlation by 0.0003 to 0.0014; moving the whole image by a pixel changes it by 0.005. The merit therefore cannot place the windows, which is why the edges are measured directly.

The stages#

Each stage is a function of esis.flights.f1.optics, and the free set of every stage is recorded in the table it writes.

  1. Absolute. fit_distortion_channel() fits each channel on its own to the reference frame, 15, with esis.optics.fit_distortion(): a seeded differential evolution over the nine terms one frame determines (grating yaw and pitch, ruling spacing, pointing pitch and yaw, and the sensor’s focus-preserving distance, roll, pitch and yaw), eighty generations of fifteen members per dimension, then a restarted Nelder-Mead polish. Every other term stays at the as-built model (esis.flights.f1.optics.as_built()).

  2. Edges. measure_window_edges() fits each side of each window’s predicted outline, in every frame whose signal is at least half the flight’s median, as one blurred straight edge: its position, slope and width are shared along the side, and each row or column crossing it has its own background, step and ramp inside, so that bright structure next to the edge cannot pull it. The crossings are medianed over the frames per row or column. The drift of the windows is read separately, from a step fit to every row and column on its own (window_drift()), which follows a side’s motion more steadily than its straight edge does. Both are referred to the reference frame: the median edges are moved by the drift there, as a cubic in the frame index smooths it, and the drift is taken from there. The middle window sits on its neighbours and is left out.

  3. Outline. fit_distortion_reference() places each channel’s windows on the edges through the grating’s yaw and pitch and the roll of the field stop, which move the windows without moving the sky within them, then re-polishes the sky terms against the merit (esis.optics.fit_distortion_outline()).

  4. Shared. The primary, the field-stop roll and the payload pitch and yaw belong to the instrument. They are set to their mean over the channels, and each channel’s grating and camera placement is polished again with them fixed.

  5. Internal. esis.optics.align_channels() samples every channel’s frame on a common sky grid through its own distortion at He I 584 and O V 630, reading only the window each line illuminates (esis.optics.window_mask()), measures the tile shifts against channel 1, and solves for the increments of the same terms whose change of the mapping reproduces the shifts. Using two lines separates a geometric error from a dispersion error.

  6. Focus. fit_defocus_history() measures every channel’s sky against channel 1 in every frame, with the windows placed by the smoothed drift, and solves the focus of every sector of the primary from the shifts through the model’s pattern, four unknowns from six measured components. A quadratic in the frame index, zero at the reference frame, smooths each sector’s history. The proxy scene takes no part: it cannot tell a focus from the internal alignment, but the channels can, to a few microns.

  7. Pointing. fit_distortion_pointing() polishes the pitch and yaw of every frame on the mean merit of the four channels, with the roll held, each channel’s grating offset by the drift of its windows and each sector focused by that frame’s value. The offsets and the focus ride along in the pointing table for distortion_fit() to apply.

  8. Field stop. The field stop is a regular octagon by design, but its image is not. measure_field_stop() measures every side of the O V window in every frame, carries the points back to the plane of the stop through the fit, and fits one shape common to the four channels after each channel’s own rotation, translation and scale are taken out. The fit is made with the stop and the stop is measured through the fit, so the two are iterated: the first pass is fit with the regular octagon, each later one with the stop the one before it measured, until a measurement turns the sides of the stop by less than 0.05 deg rms (field_stop_converged()). The regular octagon’s sides are off by about half a degree; once the stop has settled, a refit turns them again by up to 0.04 deg, the scatter of the fit itself. The offsets of the sides scatter from pass to pass by as much as the octagon is off, about a micron, so they are recorded but do not end the iteration.

  9. Acceptance. acceptance() scores the result on frames across the flight: the merit with each frame’s pointing applied on six of them, and in every frame the coalignment metric, the length of the median tile shift of each channel’s sky against channel 1 at each aligned line, its component along the channel’s dispersion, which is the part a velocity is charged for, and the scatter of the tiles about the median; with the residual of the window outlines against the measured edges. No inversion is involved.

Reproducing the committed fit#

The fit needs the Level-1 data and the AIA scene, which are downloaded and cached on first use, and more than 100 GB of memory, which loading the Level-1 frames peaks at. On one machine, from docs/reports/distortion_fit:

python reproduce.py run <dir>

runs the whole iteration and writes the tables to commit into <dir>/committed. The package holds all of them but the three only the figures read, window_edges, acceptance_tiles and acceptance_modes, which are published as a release of esis-mission/esis-data-2019 and downloaded, by hash, when they are read. It runs on the CPU by default; --device cuda runs the regridding on a GPU, which needs the gpu extra (pip install -e ".[gpu]", which adds torch) and a CUDA toolkit numba can find, and --workers spreads the capture over processes. Every stage writes its output into the pass’s directory and is skipped if the output is there, so an interrupted run continues where it stopped. The tables are compared with the committed ones by:

python reproduce.py compare <dir>/committed

On the cluster’s GPUs the capture of a channel took one to two and a half hours, the edges ten minutes, the outline, shared and internal stages one to two hours, the focus twenty minutes, the pointing of a frame ten to twenty minutes and the acceptance half an hour to an hour; the host’s CPUs took about as long for the capture. The first pass needs the four captures, later passes none, and the 30 frames’ pointing dominates them. slurm/chain.sh is an example of running the stages as jobs on a Slurm cluster, with the channels and the frames side by side; the account, partition and GPUs are the cluster’s, given in the environment it documents.

The fit is stochastic: the capture draws its population at random, and every linearization samples the field and the pupil at random positions inside its cells. One seed, --seed (0 by default), sets all of these draws, and every table records the seed and the whole environment it was made in, the interpreter, the platform, the version of every installed package and the commit of each checkout, in its header. A run repeats the committed fit to within the scatter between seeds, not exactly: on GPUs a seed repeats only to about 0.0005 in correlation. Over seeds 0, 1, 2, 4 and 5 the registration of the channels against one another varies by 0.02 px rms over the first 29 frames, against a registration of about 0.1 px, and by 0.08 px in the last, faintest frame; the parameters differ more, along the near-null directions of the problem. A capture can fall in a false optimum, a correlation of 0.65 where the others reach 0.79 to 0.85; the chain cannot continue from one, and another seed is the remedy.

Two pages judge the committed fit by eye: the blink page blinks every frame of the flight against its model (blink.py), and the coalignment page blinks any two channels read onto a common sky and shows their difference (coalign.py). They need the Level-1 frames to render and are too large for this repository, so they are published as a release of esis-mission/esis-data-2019, which docs/conf.py names and downloads, checking each page’s hash; when the tables change, the pages are rendered again and released under a new tag.

Checks#

The scripts in docs/reports/distortion_fit/checks verify choices the fit makes on a finished pass; they are not part of the fit, and nothing reads what they write but this page and the paper.

coregistration.py measures, frame by frame, what the focus of each sector leaves of the channels’ motion against one another, and fits it as an offset of each channel’s own pointing, twelve numbers through the flight; it was the measurement that led to the sector focus. On the committed fit the offsets are at most 0.11 arcsec, 0.15 px, and take the rms shift of the channels against channel 1 from 0.053 to 0.033 px. No mechanism stands behind them, so the fit does not apply them.

straight_edges.py refits a pass with the drift of the windows read from the straight edges instead of the steps of every row and column. On the committed fit the two sides of one window move apart by 0.14 to 0.39 px rms with the straight edges and by 0.06 to 0.20 px with the steps, and the steps leave the channels registered better, at 0.105 against 0.117 px rms at He I and 0.074 against 0.108 px at O V.

The focus of each sector of the primary#

With every frame’s pointing, window drift and one defocus of the whole primary applied, the channels still slid against one another through the flight: by 0.20 px rms at He I and 0.17 px at O V over the frames bright enough to measure, and by 0.6 px at the first. It was a translation of each channel’s whole image, with a tenth of a pixel of magnification and rotation at most on top; He I and O V agreed on it to a tenth of a pixel, so it was not a matter of dispersion; and it was smooth in time.

The image against the window. Against frame 15, on each detector: the motion of the image, from correlating the interior of the He I and O V windows with the same pixels at frame 15, thirty pixels in from the edges; and the motion of the window, from the edges. Taken to the sky and differenced against channel 1, the pointing drops out, as does any rigid motion of the field stop, and a grating or a camera that moved would carry its image and its window together. The image did not ride with the window: channel 2’s image stayed on channel 1’s to 0.02 px while its window moved 0.4 px, and channel 3’s image and window moved apart. So the motion was not a grating or a camera, and it was not a decenter of the field stop or of the primary, which move every window, or every image, by one vector.

The sectors. A focus that differs from sector to sector moves each channel’s image along its own dispersion by its own amount, which is a four-parameter pattern per frame against six measured components. It fits: over the flight the rms of the measured components falls from 0.20 px to 0.05 px, and in the median frame to 0.03 px, the floor of the measurement. The figure modes of the primary tried first, astigmatism, coma and trefoil, had failed because they force the four sectors into a fixed relation; the sectors want their own histories, each smooth in time to the precision of a single frame, about 3 µm. Nothing after the field stop can do this, and neither can any rigid motion of the mirror or the stop, so if it is real it is the figure of the primary changing unevenly with temperature, about 100 nm of sag between sectors over the flight.

The edges as fiducials, and the caveat. The conclusion rests on the window edges marking a rigid outline, and measured side by side and line by line in every frame they do not do so cleanly:

  • The He I window has no left edge on any detector, where the image runs off the sensor; channel 0 has no bottom edge and channel 3 no top edge.

  • The edges differ in sharpness from 0.7 to over 4 px in the width of the fitted step, with a median of 1.6, and their profiles are skewed, the dark half of the step wider than the lit by 0.8 px in the median.

  • Several edges sharpen or soften through the flight by up to 2 px, while most hold to a tenth of a pixel.

  • The two lines, which share one field stop and one grating, disagree on the motion of the same side by up to 0.3 px per fifteen frames.

The sector focus is measured along each channel’s dispersion, and the window’s position along the dispersion comes from the two edges perpendicular to it, which are the edges that behaved worst. The crossing of a skewed step moves when its width changes, and that would appear here as a focus of the sector. The disagreement between the lines puts that at up to half the effect. So the data support two readings, the mirror bending or the along-dispersion fiducials drifting, and do not decide between them; the first is the simpler physical account and is what the model carries. What would decide it: the drift measured per line with only the sharp edges; a thermal model of the mirror and its mount; and for the next flight, temperature sensors on the primary and an undispersed channel.

What remains open#

The model has no knob for a scale along \(y\) alone, and the anisotropy of channels 0 and 3 is expressed through the sensor’s yaw and distance; the physical candidate is the per-channel grating radius, which was measured and differs. The fit traces ideal materials and is not a throughput. The reference is fit to one frame and scored on the others; a polish over several frames at once is the natural next step.
